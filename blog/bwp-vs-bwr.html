BWP vs BWR Plywood — What is the difference

It is the most common question we get from architects, interior contractors, and homeowners alike: "Should I use BWP or BWR plywood for my interior project?" In this guide, we break down exactly what these terms mean and where you should use each grade.

Published by Royal Roots Expert Team • March 1, 2026 • 4 min read

1. What is BWP Plywood?

BWP stands for Boiling Waterproof. This is the highest grade of structural plywood manufactured for interior use, strictly regulated in India under the Bureau of Indian Standards code IS 710.

To achieve this certification, a plywood board is subjected to an extremely rigorous test: it must tolerate 72 continuous hours immersed in boiling water without demonstrating any signs of delamination or structural weakness. The adhesives used in BWP manufacturing are undiluted synthetic phenolic resins, creating a bond that is entirely impervious to moisture.

Ideal applications for BWP Plywood include:

  • Kitchen cabinetry, especially under-sink units
  • Bathroom vanities and partitions
  • Marine applications and boat interiors
  • Any environment subjected to high humidity, extreme moisture, or direct water contact

At Royal Roots, our premium Platinum Grade carries the IS 710 BWP certification. Manufactured with a 100% Gurjan timber core from the Nilgiris, it delivers ultimate moisture resistance and is backed by our robust 30-year warranty against any manufacturing defect.

2. What is BWR Plywood?

BWR stands for Boiling Water Resistant. This grade is certified under the IS 303 standard. While highly capable, it is important to distinguish between "waterproof" and "water-resistant."

BWR plywood is constructed to resist moisture and high ambient humidity under normal interior conditions, but it is not intended to be perpetually wet or soaked in boiling water. It utilizes a modified phenolic resin adhesive that offers excellent longevity for typical domestic and commercial environments at a more accessible price point than BWP.

Ideal applications for BWR Plywood include:

  • Living room and bedroom furniture
  • Wardrobes, bed frames, and study tables
  • TV units and wall paneling
  • Commercial retail shelving and office interior woodwork

Royal Roots offers two exceptional IS 303 BWR ranges: our Silver Grade carrying a 15-year warranty for excellent value, and our enhanced Gold Grade with a 25-year warranty, which incorporates advanced moisture-resist technology bridging the gap toward BWP performance.

3. Comparing the Grades: When to Choose Which?

Choosing between BWP and BWR comes down to accurately assessing the moisture exposure of your project.

When to choose BWP (Platinum IS 710): Use BWP whenever there is a risk of direct water exposure. For residential projects, kitchen base cabinets, bathroom vanities, and wash-area cabinetry absolutely demand BWP plywood. In commercial projects, hotel bathrooms, restaurant kitchens, and spa facilities should specify BWP uniformly to prevent long-term failure.

When to choose BWR (Silver or Gold IS 303): BWR is completely sufficient for the vast majority of dry interior spaces. Wardrobes, bed frames, and general living/study room furniture do not require boiling waterproof capabilities. Specifying BWR here saves project budget without sacrificing the structural integrity of the final furniture piece.

4. Conclusion and Next Steps

Plywood forms the structural foundation of your interior investment; if it fails, the expensive finishes applied on top sink with it. Selecting the right grade ensures decades of structural reliability.
